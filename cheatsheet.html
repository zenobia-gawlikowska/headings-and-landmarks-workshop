Screen reader cheat sheet

The keys you need for this workshop, for NVDA on Windows and VoiceOver on macOS.

NVDA (Windows)

Getting started

  • Download it free from nvaccess.org/download. A portable copy works without installing.
  • Start: Ctrl+Alt+N. Quit: NVDA+Q.
  • The NVDA key is Insert by default (or Caps Lock if you choose the laptop layout).
  • Stop speech: Ctrl. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
  • Tip: turn on Speech Viewer (NVDA menu → Tools) to see what NVDA says.

VoiceOver (macOS)

Getting started

  • Turn on and off: Cmd+F5 (or triple-press Touch ID).
  • The VO key is Control+Option (or Caps Lock if enabled in VoiceOver Utility).
  • Stop speech: Control. Use Safari for the most reliable results.
  • Tip: turn on Quick Nav (←+→ together) to use single-key navigation without VO.
  • Tip: in Safari, enable Press Tab to highlight each item (Settings → Advanced) to tab to links.

Tips for testing with a screen reader

  • Use the lists first. The headings and landmarks lists show the page’s map in seconds.
  • Listen for the level. “Heading level 4” right after “heading level 1” means a skipped level.
  • Listen for duplicates. Two landmarks both called “navigation” can’t be told apart.
  • Slow down speech while you learn, speed it up later. Nobody expects you to be fast.
  • Don’t mix up your screen reader and real users’ experience. You are checking structure, not imitating how an expert uses it.
